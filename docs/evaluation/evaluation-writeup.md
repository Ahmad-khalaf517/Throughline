# E6-S6 evaluation write-up — SCRUM-72

**Date:** 2026-09-28 UTC. **Scope:** exploratory evaluation required by BRD §§10.1–10.4. The three-brief comparison is E6-S5 / SCRUM-71; the earlier sofa-cover controlled change is E6-S4 / SCRUM-70. This report records observed results and their limits, not a new product run or a statistical estimate.

## Evidence and method

The E6-S5 briefs, fixed template, checklist, and semantic change answer keys were registered before the sample runs in commit `329f6d4` and [SCRUM-71 comment 11173](https://ahmadyaserkhalaf.atlassian.net/browse/SCRUM-71?focusedCommentId=11173). Case A, B, and C first outputs and item mappings were saved before each change in commits `5775dc5`, `f5b3445`, and `c301fc6` respectively. The final E6-S5 evidence summary is commit `3afd42d` and [SCRUM-71 comment 11206](https://ahmadyaserkhalaf.atlassian.net/browse/SCRUM-71?focusedCommentId=11206). Its source files are `docs/evaluation/three-brief-evaluation.md`, `case-{a,b,c}-proxy.md`, `case-{a,b,c}-hosted-prechange.md`, and `case-{a,b,c}-result.md` in the E6-S5 worktree at that commit. They are not present in this E6-S6 checkout, so this report reproduces the measurements needed to read the findings independently.

The fixed checklist scores eight preregistered checklist units per brief for Requirements and Backlog coverage. Some units bundle behaviors: B combines the negative-stock guard with quantity-change audit, and C combines email confirmation with signup/offer audit. An 8/8 score is coverage of those defined units, not a separate coverage measurement for each subbehavior. Functional Requirement and Story acceptance criteria are scored separately against the actual items produced. A complete criterion needs an observable action, condition, and outcome, including the relevant business rule. A valid Story link must resolve to a Requirement and have a meaningful source relationship; Architecture links are counted separately. For impact, the evaluator mapped pre-change items to the preregistered semantic answer key before applying one upstream manual edit. The changed root is excluded from downstream true-positive denominators. “False positive” below means a warning for an item whose content remains valid under that key; the product correctly presents warnings as **potential** effects and review recommendations.

The comparison condition was a **user-approved AI-assisted structured-template proxy**, authored by the same Codex agent that wrote the briefs and answer keys. It was not a human manual baseline. That author already knew the briefs and keys before timing, so its short intervals are not first-exposure completion times. The proxy's post-change impact sets were authored worksheets, not automatic system warnings. Proxy and hosted items have different identities and counts; their affected-item numbers cannot be pooled as if they were the same subjects. The hosted path included an enforced UI Requirements generation and approval step before Backlog, although the BRD names Requirements, Architecture, and Backlog as the three planning outputs.

## Interpretation and limitations

**Observed:** Throughline completed the named planning outputs in all three briefs, with 8/8 preregistered Requirements units and 8/8 Backlog-unit Story mappings per case. These unit scores do not establish coverage of every bundled subbehavior. It exposed inspectable paths for every warning it emitted. Its semantic impact sets were precise in C, included five and seven unnecessary reviews in A and B, and missed B's affected sibling Requirement. Its first-output acceptance criteria were often incomplete, and behavior-only revisions left stale or conflicting criteria. The independent sofa-cover change found both expected affected items with one unnecessary item warning, while identifying an external-reference row required extra investigation.

**Inference:** Broad dependency links and bundled Requirements appear to contribute to warning noise in A and B; sibling Requirements without a recorded dependency appear to explain B's miss. Those are interpretations of the saved item contents and paths, not proof that every future broad link or sibling relationship behaves the same way. The warning system helps a reviewer locate recorded dependencies, but still requires semantic judgment about whether an item truly needs change.

**Unverified:** The study did not establish human manual completion time, a fair speed comparison, causal productivity gains, downstream repair after warnings, or generalized impact accuracy. It did not independently audit remote Jira issue content after the E6-S4 change. The E6-S1 P0 checklist remains mixed rather than all green, and the production security check did not scan nonexistent generated GitHub/Stitch assets. No new hosted runs or P0 retests were performed for this write-up.

This is an exploratory **n = 3** comparison with one developer/evaluator, in a small capstone setting. The same evaluator prepared the keys, authored the AI-assisted proxy, operated the hosted product, and scored the outputs. The proxy's advance knowledge and the hosted extra UI gate make timing especially non-comparable. Cases have different generated item sets and answer-key denominators; even within the three hosted projects, adding counts would describe only these reviewed subjects, not a general recall or precision rate. E6-S4 is an additional distinct test, not a fourth sample brief. **These findings are not evidence of product-market fit.**
